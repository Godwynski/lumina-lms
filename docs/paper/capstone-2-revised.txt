Library Book Reservation, Borrowing, Inventory Management, QR Based Scanning
Lumina, a library book reservation, borrowing, and inventory management system are crucial aspects of academic administration in STI College Alabang, ensuring that educational institutions operate efficiently while meeting the needs of the faculty and students. This capstone project aims to explore the challenges and solutions in creating optimized tracking that balances book availability, borrowing queues, inventory counts, and conflict resolutions.
The study also evaluates QR-based scanning integration to prevent manual data entry errors while ensuring a balanced distribution of library resources.
By leveraging web hosting and securing a dedicated domain, the project aims to establish a widely secure platform for day-to-day operations. The adoption of a web-based application framework aligns with institutional standards, providing a polished and professional interface for all users.
The primary objective is to offer a system that supports strategic tasks, improves utilization, enhances student learning experience, and addresses the academic needs of students and faculty.
INTRODUCTION
Project Context
The way information is managed continues to change as technology becomes more advanced, yet many school libraries still face challenges in organizing and maintaining their resources at STI College Alabang, the library serves a large number of students from different academic programs and faculty members, each needing access to various books, references, and learning materials every day. Because of this high demand, the library needs a system that can efficiently track and manage all resources.
At present, the library still uses manual methods such as writing borrowed books in logbooks or recording them in spreadsheets. Librarians also check shelves manually to see if books are available. These methods take a lot of time and can lead to mistakes. Records may not always be updated right away, information can be misplaced, and handwriting may be difficult to read. Because of this, students sometimes have a hard time finding the books they need, and librarians struggle to keep accurate records.
This problem is not only seen in STI College Alabang but also in many schools that still use traditional library systems. As the number of students and books increases, it becomes harder to manage all transactions properly. Books may be returned late, misplaced, or even lost without proper tracking. Over time, this can lead to incorrect inventory records, which affects how the library operates.
To solve these problems, this study proposes a web-based library book reservation, borrowing, and inventory management system with QR-based scanning for STI College Alabang. The system is designed to make library transactions faster and easier. Students and faculty can reserve books online, while librarians can manage records through a single system. QR codes will be used to quickly identify books and process borrowing and returning.
With QR-based scanning, each physical book copy is given a unique code that can be scanned using a device. This makes the process faster and reduces human error. It also allows the system to update records in real time, helping ensure that the inventory is always accurate.
The system also aims to improve convenience for both students and librarians. Students will no longer need to manually search for book availability, as they can easily check and reserve materials through the system. Librarians, on the other hand, can spend less time on manual encoding and focus more on assisting students.
In addition, the system helps improve data safety and organization. Digital records are easier to store, update, and back up compared to manual records. It also helps reduce the risk of losing important information and ensures that only authorized users can access or modify data.
Lastly, the system can help the library make better decisions. With organized data, librarians can see which books are most used, which materials need updating, and how resources are being used overall. This helps improve the library’s services and better support student needs.
Purpose and Description
This project provides STI College Alabang with a streamlined digital library management system that replaces manual processes with efficient QR code scanning and online book reservations. Designed for reliability, the user-friendly platform centralizes library tasks into one accessible web interface, ensuring that data is managed effectively and operations remain consistent. By modernizing circulation and inventory tracking, the system enhances data security and provides a more organized, seamless experience for students, faculty, and library personnel. In the system, Student Assistants use the "Library Staff" role, while Students and faculty patrons use the "Patron" role.
Statement of the Problem
General Problem
The primary challenge of this research is to design, develop, and implement a web-based library management system for STI College Alabang that replaces manual or fragmented processes with a unified digital framework. It integrates Microsoft O365 authentication and QR-based identification to support automatic reservations, staff-processed borrowing and returns, overdue fine recording and settlement, and inventory management for students, faculty, and library administrators.
Specific Problem
Super Admin
How to implement a secure login module using Microsoft O365 authentication for Super Administrator?
In this module, Super Administrator can access the system using their institutional accounts to verify their identity. This ensures that only authorized personnel can access sensitive library administrative tools and data through a standardized sign-in process.
How to develop a centralized dashboard module that provides a real-time overview of daily library operations for super administrators?
In this module, super administrators can monitor key metrics such as Today's Visitors, Active Borrows, Inventory (physical copies), and total users, plus an Action Center (card applications, overdue borrows, ready holds), live activity feed, recently cataloged books, and their Library Card; Analytics and Audit Logs open from the sidebar.
How to design an efficient inventory module that enables automated and batch cataloging operations for the super administrator?
In this module, the super administrator can manage the library's collection by automatically fetching book details via ISBN lookup and manually finalizing storage and classification records. It supports spreadsheet and background batch ISBN imports (up to 500 books), auto-generated accession numbers, QR label printing, per-copy acquisition/disposal tracking, and customizable exports.
How to automate the borrowing and returning process using QR-code scanning technologies for the circulation desk?
In this module, the system supports optional camera/handheld scanning or typed entry: checkout confirms patron card and copy IDs; return uses only the copy ID. Self-checkout is blocked. Overdue/lost items or unpaid fines block loans unless a Librarian or Super Admin overrides with a recorded reason; fines are paid in person or stay Receivable.
How to create a Borrowing Logs module that allows the Super Admin to monitor and filter book transactions and settle overdue fines?
In this module, the super administrator can track library items through an interactive log showing borrower details, book info, loan timelines, and overdue penalties. It provides search, All/Active/Overdue/Returned/Settlements tabs, and Return & Settle or Settle actions (including condoning with remarks).
How to develop an analytics module with data visualization tools to track system trends and library performance for the super administrator?
In this module, the super administrator can filter performance metrics across preset or custom ranges to review Visits, Loans Issued, physical-copy Inventory, and Active Loans. It provides Peak Visiting Hours (bar), Circulation Trends (line), and Genre Popularity (donut) charts, a demand leaderboard, Excel export/print, and a Report Builder (PDF/Excel/CSV).
How to implement a comprehensive user directory module that enables the super administrator to manage and modify registered user accounts?
In this module, the super administrator can view all accounts with names, IDs, roles, and student academic programs, with search (name, email, student ID) and filters. Profile pages allow verifying and approving pending accounts, granting Student Assistant permissions, setting statuses (Active/Pending/Inactive/Suspended/Archived), and transferring Super Admin ownership.
How to implement an attendance logs module using card-scanning technology to monitor patron traffic within the library?
In this module, the super administrator can track library usage by selecting Check-In or Check-Out mode and scanning library cards or typing card numbers/student IDs. It logs entry and exit timestamps, auto-closes forgotten exits at closing time as "No Log Out," and supports archiving records older than one year.
How to develop a centralized system policies module that enables the super administrator to manage operational rules and backend configurations?
In this module, the super administrator can set circulation/reservation limits, fine rates, categories, academic programs, dashboard FAQs (requiring change reasons), and audience-targeted announcements. It also provides Super Admin lifecycle tools: annual patron resets, archiving records older than one year (after Excel backup), maintenance, and test emails.
How to implement a secure audit logs module that tracks, filters, and exports system activities for administrative accountability?
In this module, the super administrator can access a security tracking system that records timestamps, user identities, and technical differences for key administrative and circulation actions, with search, entity, action, subject, and date filters, and CSV, JSON, and Excel exports.
How to design an efficient notification module that alerts the super administrator about critical system events and updates in real time?
In this module, the super administrator can access recent alerts instantly through a header bell dialog or a dedicated notifications page with All/Unread/History tabs, search, Circulation/Reservation/System filters, mark-all-read, and clear-read controls.
How to create a secure profile module with read-only account details and profile photo controls for logged-in users?
In this module, authenticated users can review their own institutional and contact information including full name, system ID, official email, and an Account Access note. Approved details are read-only; Pending users can update onboarding info via Edit Info, and all users can upload, crop, change, or remove their photo.

OBJECTIVES
General Objectives
To design and develop Lumina, a web-based library book reservation, borrowing, and inventory management system with QR-based scanning for STI College Alabang. The system aims to enhance efficiency in library transactions through a centralized online platform, improve inventory tracking accuracy, and provide seamless real-time access to library services for students, faculty, and library staff.
Specific Objectives
Super Admin
To integrate a secure login module using Microsoft O365 authentication for identity verification.
This objective ensures a secure and standardized sign-in process by leveraging institutional accounts, verifying that only authorized personnel can access administrative tools.
To develop a Centralized Dashboard Module that displays a real-time overview of daily library operations, critical system metrics, an Action Center, and recent activity.
This objective aims to provide super admin with a centralized, high-level control panel of Today's Visitors, Active Borrows, Inventory (physical copies), and total users—plus an Action Center (card applications, overdue borrows, ready holds), live activity feed, recently cataloged books, and their Library Card.
To develop an Inventory Module that enables Super administrators to manage the library’s book collection through automated ISBN fetching and detailed manual cataloging.
This objective lets the Super Admin fetch book details via ISBN lookup, fill missing Dewey Decimal numbers, and set categories, copies, and shelf locations, with background batch ISBN imports, auto-generated accession numbers, QR label printing, and per-copy acquisition/disposal records.
To develop a Circulation Desk Module that automates the library's borrowing and returning processes utilizing QR-code scanning technologies.
This objective aims to streamline front-desk operations by allowing staff to scan or type patron card and copy IDs, confirm checkouts, and use copy IDs alone for returns and fine recording. Scope and Limitations
Technology has changed the way libraries work, making it much faster to track books and share information compared to old paper methods. Instead of searching through boxes of cards, computers now let us check if a book is available in just a few seconds. This project follows that trend by bringing digital tools to the STI College Alabang library.

Login Module
This module provides a secure and standardized entry point for library staff using Microsoft O365 authentication. It restricts access to administrative tools to ensure that only authorized personnel can manage library data.

This module provides a centralized, high-level control panel that gives Super administrators a real-time overview of daily operations and system metrics. Limitation
The system requires an active internet connection to synchronize data and operate its cloud-based features.
System capacity and performance are restricted by the database size, serverless execution bounds, and storage limits of the chosen free-tier cloud infrastructure.
Automated fetching of book data via ISBN is often incomplete, requiring librarians to manually enter missing metadata such as Dewey Decimal call numbers when external sources do not supply them (accession numbers are generated automatically).
Authentication is exclusively tied to Microsoft 365 institutional accounts, which prevents access for outside researchers or users without an official school email.
The QR-based automation supports optional camera or handheld scanning and typed identifiers. If a camera is unavailable, a handheld scanner or typed card number, student ID, or copy accession number can be used; unreadable printed codes can be bypassed with typed identifiers.
The searchable catalog focuses strictly on the library's physical book collection, including titles with copies on loan, meaning it does not integrate with enterprise-grade electronic research databases like EBSCO.
The application is constrained by external API rate limits and real-time synchronization quotas, which may restrict the volume of requests processed during peak library hours.

Global Shift Toward Digital Library Transformation
The rapid evolution of digital technologies has significantly impacted how libraries operate, leading to a shift in library services locally and internationally. Despite the widespread acknowledgment of this digital shift, there remains a substantial gap in understanding of how academic libraries are adapting, specifically in integrating digital innovations to enhance user access and engagement. In the study of Distanont et al (2024), there is a critical global trend toward integrating digital innovations to enhance user engagement. This shift is not merely about digitizing records but about strategic adaptation to a landscape where users expect seamless access. METHODOLOGY
This project uses the Iterative Development method to build and set up A Web-Based Library Book Reservation, Borrowing, and Inventory Management System with QR-Based Scanning for STI College Alabang. We chose this approach for one main reason: it allows us to build the system and write our research paper at the same time. Instead of waiting to finish all the paperwork before writing code, this method lets us work on small parts of both tasks together. This helps us make steady progress and ensures our written report always matches the real, working system.
Stage 1 Planning
In this first step, we plan the main features we want to create for a specific part of the library system, such as the login page or the QR scanner. At the same time, we write down these initial plans and requirements directly into our project document.
Stage 2 Design
Next, we design how that specific part of the system will look and work. We map out the user screens and database layouts for that feature. As soon as the design is ready, we add these sketches and diagrams to our paper.
Stage 3 Implementation
This is the step where we actually write the code and build the feature we just designed, like creating the book inventory table. Working this way allows us to draft the technical descriptions in our manuscript while the coding is fresh in our minds.
Stage 4 Testing
Once the feature is coded, we test it carefully to make sure there are no errors. We check that the QR codes scan properly and that books can be borrowed without issues. All the test results and fixes are written straight into our report.
Stage 5 Evaluation
In this final step of the cycle, we look at the tested feature to see what works and what needs to be fixed. Once this part is reviewed and finalized, we update our paper to match it perfectly, and then we start the cycle all over again for the next system feature.

Overview of current Technologies to be used in the system
The system is built upon a modern, high-performance tech stack designed for scalability, security, and a premium user experience. At its core, the application utilizes Next.js 16, a powerful React 19 framework that enables server-side rendering and optimized routing, ensuring fast load times and seamless navigation. This foundation is reinforced by TypeScript, which provides a robust type system to catch errors early and maintain high code quality across the entire development lifecycle. The combination of these core technologies allows for a highly responsive and maintainable codebase that can adapt to evolving requirements.
For backend infrastructure, the system leverages Supabase, an open-source Backend-as-a-Service that provides a managed PostgreSQL database, integrated authentication, and real-time data synchronization. This allows the system to handle complex data relationships and user security with minimal overhead, utilizing Row-Level Security (RLS) to ensure data integrity at the database level. The backend is further supported by Node.js 24 with npm 11, providing a scalable runtime environment for executing server-side logic and managing asynchronous operations efficiently.
The user interface is crafted with a focus on aesthetics and accessibility, utilizing Tailwind CSS for rapid, utility-first styling and Radix UI for unstyled, accessible component primitives. Fluid animations and interactive transitions are powered by Framer Motion, while Lucide React provides a consistent and modern iconography set. Data visualization is handled by Recharts, offering clear and actionable insights through responsive charts. Together, these tools create a cohesive design system that feels premium, alive, and intuitive for every user.

Requirements Documentation
This specific part defines the software features of the web-based system of Lumina: A Web-Based Library Book Reservation, Borrowing, and Inventory Management System with QR-Based Scanning for STI College Alabang. The requirements stated below are formulated based on the data gathered, standard operating procedure analysis, and key stakeholder consultations conducted with the library staff and administration of STI College Alabang, as well as particularly selected information based on the specific objectives of this study. The defined requirements below are expected to cater to the operational needs of STI College Alabang to enhance library transaction efficiency, inventory tracking accuracy, and asset security. The requirements below also describe how all the authorized users Super Administrators, Librarians, Student Assistants, and Students, including faculty patrons, of STI College Alabang can interact with the system.
4.1. Functional Requirements
4.1.1. Log In (Super Admin)
REQ001. The super admin shall be able to log in securely using Microsoft O365 institutional account credentials.
REQ002. The system shall restrict access to administrative tools to ensure that only authorized super administrative personnel can manage backend data.
4.1.2. Dashboard Module (Super Admin)
REQ003. The super admin shall be able to view a real-time overview displaying metric summary counters for Today's Visitors, Active Borrows, Inventory (physical copies), and total users.
REQ004. The super admin shall view an Action Center (card applications, overdue borrows, ready holds) and live activity feed, while accessing Circulation Desk, Inventory, User Directory, Attendance Logs, Audit Logs, and Analytics via the sidebar.
REQ005. The Super Admin shall view recently cataloged books and their Library Card, and use Catalog to search (title/author/ISBN), filter/sort, check copy availability, and automatically reserve for pickup or join a waitlist.
4.1.3. Inventory (Super Admin)
REQ006. The super admin shall fetch book metadata (title, author, cover, and Dewey Decimal when available) via ISBN lookup (Google Books/Open Library).
REQ007. The Super Admin shall enter missing Dewey Decimal numbers, categories, copies, and shelf locations; book details shall provide per-copy acquisition/disposal, auto-generated accession numbers, and printable QR labels.
REQ008. The super admin shall be able to utilize a Batch Catalog Operations panel equipped with a drag-and-drop Import Sheet tool for uploading inventory spreadsheets.
REQ009. The super admin shall run background batch ISBN imports (up to 500 books) with automatic cover retrieval.
REQ010. The super admin shall be able to export active catalog data with custom column selections into Spreadsheet, CSV, JSON Data, or Covers ZIP file formats.
4.1.4. Circulation Desk (Super Admin)
REQ011. The Super Admin shall scan or type patron card and copy QR/accession IDs for checkout, and copy ID only for return (camera optional).
REQ012. Checkout shall require review and confirmation, block staff self-checkout, and block patrons with overdue/lost items or unpaid fines unless a Librarian or Super Admin overrides with a recorded reason.
4.1.5. Borrowing Logs (Super Admin)
REQ013. The Super Admin shall view non-archived loans and use Return & Settle or Settle for in-person payment or condoning (with remarks); records older than one year may be archived.
REQ014. The super admin shall view borrower name/ID, book title/author, borrow/due/return dates, and overdue penalty.
REQ015. The super admin shall view loan tags (Active, Returned, Overdue) and fine statuses (Receivable, Paid, Condoned).
REQ016. The super admin shall search loans and filter by All, Active, Overdue, Returned, or Settlements tabs.
4.1.6. Analytics (Super Admin)
REQ017. The super admin shall filter analytics by 7 days, 30 days, 1 year, or custom dates, and export to Excel or print.
REQ018. The super admin shall be able to view summary counters for Visits, Loans Issued, Inventory (physical copies), and Active Loans.
REQ019. The super admin shall be able to view a Peak Visiting Hours bar chart and a Circulation Trends line graph.
REQ020. The super admin shall be able to view a donut chart displaying Genre Popularity across borrowed catalog categories.
REQ021. The Super Admin shall view top-borrowed books and build up to 8-section reports (Attendance, Borrowing, Inventory, Users) as PDF, Excel, or CSV.
4.1.7. User Directory (Super Admin)
REQ022. The super admin shall view all accounts with names, emails, IDs, roles, statuses, and student academic programs.
REQ023. The super admin shall search accounts by name, email, or student ID and apply filters.
REQ024. The super admin shall be able to click on any user entry to open their detailed profile page and inspect contact information.
REQ025. The super admin shall verify and approve pending accounts, change roles, grant Student Assistant permissions (manage_inventory, manage_circulation, manage_attendance), set statuses (Active/Pending/Inactive/Suspended/Archived), and transfer Super Admin ownership.
4.1.8. Attendance Logs (Super Admin)
REQ026. The super admin shall log patron visits in Check-In/Check-Out mode via card QR scans or typed card/student IDs.
REQ027. The Super Admin shall view entry/exit timestamps; forgotten exits close at closing time as "No Log Out," and records older than one year may be archived.
4.1.9. System Policies (Super Admin)
REQ028. The super admin shall configure loan limits (default 14 days, 5 books), hold limits (default 3 holds, 3-day pickup), and fine rate (default PHP 5/day).
REQ029. The super admin shall be able to configure student academic programs and organize catalog book categories.
REQ030. The super admin shall publish announcements to Patrons & Library Staff or all users.
REQ031. The super admin shall manage dashboard Support & FAQs, recording a required reason for every policy or FAQ change.
REQ032. The super admin shall run lifecycle tools: annual resets suspending all patrons (students and faculty), archiving records older than one year (after Excel backup), maintenance, and test emails.
4.1.10. Audit Logs (Super Admin)
REQ033. The super admin shall view an audit log of key administrative/circulation actions (catalog, copies, policies, users, circulation, fines, resets) with timestamps, actors, entities, and descriptions.
REQ034. The super admin shall be able to click a dedicated button on log entries to view granular technical differences (diffs).
REQ035. The super admin shall be able to search audit logs by name, action type, or reason and filter logs by entity, action, subject, and date range.
REQ036. The super admin shall be able to export audit log records in CSV, JSON, and Excel formats.
4.1.11. Notification (Super Admin)
REQ037. The super admin shall be able to click a header notification bell icon to view a modal dialog displaying recent system alerts.
REQ038. The super admin shall use a notifications page (All/Unread/History tabs, search, Circulation/Reservation/System filters, mark-all-read, clear-read); a daily Nodemailer job emails due-soon/overdue reminders.
4.1.12. My Profile (Super Admin)
REQ039. The super admin shall view read-only account details (name, ID, email, contact, address, status, role, Account Access note) and upload, crop, change, or remove their photo.
4.1.13. Log In (Librarian)

4.2. Non-Functional Requirements
4.2.1. Performance
REQ079. The system shall process and display real-time transaction updates (such as QR-based borrowing, returning, and entry/exit card scans) immediately upon scanning.
REQ080. The system shall maintain smooth response times for concurrent user activities (such as simultaneous catalog browsing, reservation holds, and administrative reporting) without performance degradation.
REQ081. The system shall load search query results, inventory filtering options, and analytics visualizations without noticeable interface lag.
4.2.2. Availability
REQ082. The system shall remain operationally accessible 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, subject to planned maintenance and Microsoft O365, Vercel, and Supabase service availability.
4.2.3. Security
REQ083. The system must enforce user authentication exclusively through Microsoft O365 institutional accounts across all user roles (Super Admin, Librarian, Student Assistant, Student) to prevent unauthorized access.
REQ084. The system must enforce strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), blocking Students from administrative tools, Audit Logs, and directories; Student Assistant operational access requires an Active account and the relevant Super Admin permission.
REQ085. The system must maintain an access-controlled audit trail of key administrative actions (catalog, copies, policies, users, circulation, fines, resets).
4.2.4. Usability
REQ086. The system must feature an intuitive, visually clear, and consistent interface layout tailored specifically to each of the four distinct user roles.
REQ087. The system shall utilize consistent color-coded visual badges (e.g., green for Returned, blue/yellow for Active, red for Overdue) across all tables to optimize operational readability.
REQ088. The system shall provide quick-access keyword search bars and dropdown filters across all catalog, directory, log, and notification interfaces.
4.2.5. Maintainability
REQ089. The system architecture shall be modularly designed to allow updates to system rules, borrowing configurations, or academic program lists without disrupting core circulation services.
4.2.6. Portability and Deployment
REQ090. The system must function consistently across major modern web browsers, including Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, Microsoft Edge, and Apple Safari, without requiring special extensions.
REQ091. The system shall be fully accessible and responsive across multiple hardware devices and operating systems (Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android).
REQ092. The system shall support customizable data export operations, exporting catalog data and audit logs as CSV, JSON, or Excel, plus cover ZIP for inventory.
Design of Software, System, Product, and/or Processes
In designing our software, system, and process, we utilized structured architectural and design approaches to create a secure, efficient, and user-centered system. The system follows a layered architecture consisting of the Presentation, Process/Logic, Database Client, and Database Core layers, ensuring clear separation of responsibilities and maintainability. Database design applies Third Normal Form (3NF) to minimize redundancy and maintain data integrity, while ACID transactions, row-level locking, advisory locks, and database triggers support reliable and concurrent library operations. Security was incorporated through authentication, role-based access control (RBAC), input validation using Zod, Row Level Security (RLS), restricted database procedures, and audit logging of key administrative actions. The system also applies ISO/IEC 25010 quality attributes, focusing on functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and portability. Integrating these architectural, database, security, and quality design practices resulted in a robust, scalable, and user-friendly library management product.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Testing
In this part, the proponents present the testing activities conducted for Lumina: A Web-Based Library Book Reservation, Borrowing, and Inventory Management System with QR-Based Scanning for STI College Alabang. Testing focuses on verifying whether the developed modules and functions operate according to their intended requirements. The system follows the Iterative Development method, where each feature is tested after implementation to identify errors and necessary improvements. The testing includes Microsoft O365 authentication, book searching and reservation, QR-based scanning, borrowing and returning, fines and in-person settlement, attendance monitoring, inventory management, notifications, and administrative functions, with the results used to improve the system in the next development cycle.
Description of Prototype
The prototype is a web-based library management system designed to provide students, faculty, and library personnel with a centralized platform for accessing and managing library services. It includes features such as book searching and reservation, borrowing and returning, fines and in-person settlement, inventory management, attendance monitoring, notifications, and user profile management. The system uses Next.js, TypeScript, Supabase, Tailwind CSS, and HTML5-QRCode to support its web interface, database, and QR-based scanning functions. Overall, the prototype is designed to improve the efficiency of library transactions, organize book records, and provide convenient access to library services for students, faculty, and library personnel.
Implementation Plan
The implementation of  A Web-Based Library Book Reservation, Borrowing, and Inventory Management System with QR-Based Scanning for STI College Alabang resulted in a centralized platform that supports book searching, reservations, borrowing and returning, inventory management, and attendance monitoring. Students and faculty can search and reserve books, view borrowing history and due dates, check attendance records, receive notifications, and access their profiles. Library personnel can manage book records and transactions, including fines and in-person settlement, using QR scans or typed identifiers, while administrators can manage inventory, borrowing logs, analytics, users, System Policies, and Audit Logs. Overall, the implemented features provide an organized digital platform that supports library operations, improves access to information, and reduces reliance on manual recording.
Implementation Results
The implementation of A Web-Based Library Book Reservation, Borrowing, and Inventory Management System with QR-Based Scanning for STI College Alabang resulted in a functional system that integrates the major library processes into one centralized web-based platform. The implemented modules allow students and faculty to browse available books, reserve books and borrow through authorized staff at the desk, view borrowing history, view read-only profile details, and manage their profile photos, while administrators can manage book records, monitor inventory, process borrowing, returns, and in-person fine settlements, and manage user transactions. The QR-based scanning feature was also implemented to support faster and more accurate identification of books and borrowing transactions. Overall, the implementation results demonstrate that the developed system successfully addresses the identified limitations of the existing manual processes by providing a more organized, accessible, and efficient approach to library management.
CONCLUSION
The development and implementation of Lumina directly addresses the longstanding inefficiencies associated with manual library operations at STI College Alabang. By replacing time-consuming paper logbooks and manual shelf checks with a centralized, web-based platform, the project unifies book reservation, borrowing, circulation, and inventory auditing into a cohesive digital ecosystem.
Through the integration of QR-based scanning technologies, Microsoft O365 enterprise authentication, and a flexible iterative development methodology, Lumina ensures real-time data accuracy, minimizes administrative wait times, and safeguards institutional records. The modular system design fosters an intuitive, user-centric environment that simplifies daily tasks for administrators, librarians, student assistants, students, and faculty alike. Ultimately, transitioning from traditional record-keeping to a modern, cloud-based digital framework resolves immediate operational bottlenecks while laying a scalable, future-ready foundation. This transformation directly supports STI College Alabang's broader commitment to academic excellence, data integrity, and digital innovation in library management.
RecommendationThe researchers conducted a complete evaluation of the system and identified advanced areas for system expansion, specifically AI-Driven Predictive Shelf-Life and Condition Forecasting, Automated Data-Anonymized CSV/JSON Audit Exporters for Accreditation, and Offline Queueing for Mobile QR Scanning .
The proponents propose the following recommendation: 1.AI-Driven Predictive Shelf-Life and Condition Forecasting  The integration of a machine learning regression model within the management information system (MIS) will optimize physical asset preservation. By tracking how often a specific physical book is circulated, its age, and historical damage logs, the system can flag textbooks that are at high risk of structural wear, allowing librarians to proactively plan for repairs, rebinding, or replacements before the material becomes unusable.
2.Automated Data-Anonymized CSV/JSON Audit Exporters for Accreditation
Implementing an automated compliance module within the super-admin panel will greatly simplify institutional evaluation processes. The system can generate encrypted, data-anonymized reports in both CSV and JSON formats that demonstrate precise library utilization metrics, book-to-student ratios, and inventory turn rates over specific academic years without compromising student privacy or data integrity controls.
3. Queueing for Mobile QR Scanning.
Give librarians and student assistants who perform inventories in remote areas of the library or have poor internet connectivity offline access so they can continue scanning book QR codes; once a stable internet link is established, the scanned data and transaction logs will be automatically synchronized with the Supabase database.
